It's a battle tested standard that renders natively in browsers without modification. There are a couple things to recommend against it, though. -- numbered 1. It's not friendly to writing by hand. Unless you're hard-core it's not something most folks are into. Tooling would be necessary to convert content from plain-text into HTML before storage. 2. It's tough to standardize content types. Microformats^^5^^ were an attempt. They never took off. That's at least partially because they lock the data into the rendering layer. Sure, you can do all kinds of things with CSS, but you have to set up a parser if you want to use the data independently. -- p Markdown is another option. It's easy to learn and already has huge market share in the developer community. It's great for blog posts, but kinda sucks for anything beyond. (I mean, you can always roll your own HTML, but that justs add the complexity of dealing with HTML on to the handling. Markdown also lacks the ability to add metadata to content. For example, say you to apply a custom style to one of the ``H2`` elements on a page. There's no mechanism to do that in native Markdown.
