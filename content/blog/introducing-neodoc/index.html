But, damned if it's not perfect the Atmosphere. -- comment You're seeing the raw format right now. That's because the parser hasn't been integrated into the site yet. That's no problem. NeoDoc is designed to make it easy to make content. A big part of that is ensuring it's readable as plain-text. It's like >>Markdown| https://www.markdownguide.org/>>. But, you can do way more with it. For example, when NeoDoc files are transformed into web pages the ``-- comment`` sections are hidden. So, this note will automatically disappear when the parser is in place. Appropriate, since you'll no longer be seeing the raw text at that point. -- h2 How It Started Three years ago, I hit my breaking point with website tools. I was using Next.js^^next^^ and tried to correct a typo on my site. I changed a single character and everything broke. Some part of the process that built the site simply stopped working. My site is 25 years old. I have no desire to deal with that shit for the next 25. So, I started building my own tools. Since I had a blank slate, I re-examined every aspect. That included using Markdown for content. -- footnote -- id: next Look. I know lots of folks love >>Next|https://nextjs.org/>> and >>React|https://react.dev/>>. They're just way overkill for what I do. The associated complexity causes more issue than I'm willing to deal with. -- h2 Starting From Scratch One of my favorite phrases is: -- blockquote Broken gets fixed. Shoddy is forever. -- p As great as Markdown is for simple blog posts, it sucks at making interesting pages. Anything beyond basic formatting and you have to roll to using HTML directly. For the longest time, I considred it a shoddy experience. As I thought about making content for the rest of my life, I realized it's broken^^3^^. -- footnote -- id: 3 Or, at least, the wrong tool for the job. When you can't do interesting things in HTML without having to actually write the HTML, it's not up to snuff. -- p That realiztion started me down the path of desining a new content format. Something as easy to use as Markdown without sacraficing the power of the web's native language. Three years later, the result is NeoDoc. -- h2 Where It's At The NeoDoc parser is currently embedded in my static site generator. Extracting it has been on the todo list forever. It just wasn't a priority. Folks would have to make new tools to use it. Not likely, given the quality of the established website building options. But, you know what doesn't have established options? The Atmosphere. It's the Wild West. Harkenoning back to the early days of the web. Before everything got forced into the conformity of corporste, siloed social networks. We're at an epoch. Standardization is still under way. Once it takes hold, it'll be impossible to overcome. If we're not careful, we'll end up with Markdown and siloed content again. It's up to us to make sure that doesn't happen. To take our best shots at improving things before they solidify. -- h2 Taking A Swing And so, I'm offeing NeoDoc as a path forward. Not just for websites, but as an interoperatable format to underlie the Atmosphere. The Personal Data Servers of AT Proto give us control of our content. We can move what we make between apps at our own volition. That's of little use if they all require differnet formats. The promise of freedom of movement is only avaialbe if movemnet requires no effort. That's what NeoDoc offers. The final piece of puzzle to complete the vision of the decentralized web. -a -- endnote I use NeoDoc on my personal site where it's responsible for >>three thousand or so pages| https://www.alanwsmith.com/neo-files/report>>. -- metadata -- created: 2026-07-27T19:17:00-04:00 -- status: done -- tags: NeoDoc == endfilter == endblock
