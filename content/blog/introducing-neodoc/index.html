I'm Alan. I'd like to introduce you to NeoDoc. A writing format for the Atmosphere^^1^^. -- comment You're seeing the raw format of this post right now. That's because the NeoDoc parser hasn't been integrated into the site yet. That's no problem. NeoDoc was designed to make creating content easy. A big part of that was ensuring it's easy to read too. It's like >>Markdown| https://www.markdownguide.org/>>. But, you can do way more with it. For example, when NeoDoc files are transformed into web pages the ``-- comment`` sections are hidden. So, this note will automatically disappear when the parser is in place. Appropriate, since you'll no longer be seeing the raw text at that point. -- h2 How It Started Three years ago, I hit my breaking point with website tools. I was using Next.js^^2^^ and tried to correct a typo on my site. I changed a single character and everything broke. Some part of the process that built the site simply stopped working. My site is 25 years old. I have no desire to deal with that shit for the next 25. So, I started building my own tools. Since I had a blank slate, I re-examined every aspect. That included using Markdown for content. -- h2 From Scratch One of my favorite phrases is: -- blockquote/ Broken gets fixed. Shoddy is forever. -- /blockquote As great as Markdown is for simple blog posts, it sucks at making interesting pages. Anything beyond basic formatting and you have to roll your own HTML. For the longest time, I considered it a shoddy experience. As I thought about making content for the rest of my life, I realized it's broken. Or, at least, it's the wrong tool for the job. When you can't do interesting things in HTML without having to actually write the HTML, it's not up to snuff. The realization started me down the path of designing a new content format. Something as easy to use as Markdown without sacrificing the power of the web's native language. Three years later, NeoDoc is the result. -- h2 Where It's At The NeoDoc parser is currently embedded in my static site generator. Extracting it has been on the todo list forever. It just wasn't a priority. Folks would have to make new tools to use it. Not likely, given the quality of the established website building options. But, you know what doesn't have established options yet? **The Atmosphere**. It's the Wild West. Harkening back to the early days of the web. Before everything was jammed into the conformity of corporate, siloed social networks. It's awesome. -- h2 Taking A Swing We're at an epoch. Standards haven't been set. Once they have, they'll be insurmountable. Thanks to AT Proto, we don't have to worry about siloed content. We can move what we make at our own volition. But, if we're not careful, we'll end up with Markdown again. Or, worse. We'll end up with no standard at all. The promise of the freedom of movement will be lost to a cacophony of content formats. Apps unintentionally locking us in because no one else is set up process content the way they store it. And so, I'm offering NeoDoc as a path forward. Not just for websites, but as an interoperable format to underlie the Atmosphere. A human readable way to make sure we can make the stuff we make go where we want. If that sounds like the kinda thing you're into, you can follow along >>on this site|/>> or >>on Bluesky |https://bsky.app/profile/neodoc.style>>.
