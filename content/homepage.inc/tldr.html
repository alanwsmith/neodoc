Each one starts with two dashes and a name. For example, there's a single "__-- title__" and two "__-- book-review__" sections in the example above. (The book-review sections also have a couple lines of attributes after the header. We'll get to those momentarily.) NeoDoc generates HTML by matching section names to templates. That's how each of the hypothetical apps above created their view of the content. However, there's no guarantee that any given app will have a template for a specific section type. NeoDoc handles those cases by falling back to rendering sections without templates as paragraphs. So, an app without __-- title__ or __-- book-review__ templates would render the monthly review post like this: == endfilter == include dir("/examples/book-review-3/content.html") == filter md ### The Attributes Each of the __-- book-review__ sections in the example has additions lines starting with two dashes. Specifically, the __-- author__ and __-- cover__ lines. These are _section attributes_. Each one consists of a key/value pair. Attribute content doesn't appear by default That's why the authors and cover images don't show up in the fallback, paragraph view of the content. It's up to individual apps to include the content or not as they see fit. But, attributes offer more than just the ability to appear in the output. They provide data that can be queried. ## The NeoDoc Database NeoDoc's sections and attributes create the foundation of queryable, ad hoc database. For example, say you have a piece of content with a hundred __-- book-review__ sections in it. Each one with an __-- author__ attribute. You could write an app that lets you search for and display all the apps from a given author. There's no reason the query has to be limited to a single file. The app could be set up to query against all your posts looking for any __-- book-review__ with an __-- author__ of the given name. Going the next step up, there's no reason the app has to be limited to just _your_ content. With the public, open nature of the Atmosphere, you can query everything that's in any NeoDoc out there. == endfilter
